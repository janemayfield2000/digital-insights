Free vs. Paid SaaS Directory Listings: How to Decide When to Spend Money

Almost every major software directory offers a free listing, and almost every one also sells a paid upgrade. For a SaaS team with a limited budget, the question is not whether paid placement exists. It is whether paying makes sense yet, and on which platform.

This article walks through how the cost models differ, the traps to avoid, and a simple process that ties spending to evidence.

Start With the Free Tier

The SaaS directory listing overview shows that most major platforms let you begin at no cost:

  • G2: listing your product is free, and a complete profile works as your storefront.
  • Capterra: a free listing includes a basic profile and review collection.
  • GetApp: the basic plan is free and includes a basic profile and review collection.
  • Software Advice: claiming a basic profile is free, and a free program collects reviews for you.
  • TrustRadius: a free profile covers product details, features, visuals, and pricing.

A free profile is not a lesser option. It is a test. If a complete, honest, well-categorized free profile cannot produce engaged visitors, paying for placement will most likely amplify a weak result rather than fix it.

Understand How Each Platform Makes Money

Paid models differ, and knowing the logic behind them helps you judge whether they fit your goals.

  • G2 sells intent data. The overview reports entry-level programs starting around $10,000 per year, with top placement in a category costing a few thousand dollars per quarter.
  • Capterra sells clicks to marketers. Paid placement is pay-per-click, with entry-level spending described as a few hundred dollars per month.
  • GetApp and Software Advice offer premium tiers for more visibility and leads, plus extras such as demo videos.
  • TrustRadius offers premium profiles with lead capture and competitive analytics, while keeping its “No Ads, No Bias” policy, so placement cannot be bought.
  • BetaList offers a paid fast-track at $129 for teams that do not want to wait six weeks or more in the free queue.
  • AppSumo works on revenue splits rather than listing fees, with the plans reported ranging from 95% to the SaaS company down to 30%.

These figures come from the overview and may change, so always confirm current pricing directly with the platform before budgeting.

Know When You Are Ready to Pay

Paid options make more sense when your free profile is complete and verified against your source copy, you already have recent reviews and a steady way to collect more, and referral traffic shows engaged sessions rather than just clicks. You should also be able to see assisted conversion signals from the platform, and your team should be able to handle a surge of leads without dropping quality.

Hold off when the profile is thin or still has empty fields, when product details have changed and listings are out of date, or when you have no way to measure results beyond raw clicks. Also hold off if the platform does not match your buyers. A tool for solo freelancers gains little from a platform built around enterprise buyers. In those cases, spend the time improving the profile instead of the budget.

Use a Scoring Model to Avoid Prestige Spending

Famous platforms are tempting, but fame is not a strategy. The 2026 guide to SaaS business listing sites recommends scoring every candidate on segment fit, trust quality, asset depth, conversion path, keep-up speed, local relevance, and capacity fit. Platforms scoring 24 or more out of 35 are launch candidates, those scoring 20 to 23 belong in test mode, and anything lower is de-prioritized.

Apply the same logic to spending. A platform that scores well on fit and shows engagement on its free tier is a better candidate for a paid upgrade than one you simply recognize.

Set Rules Before You Pay

Before spending anything, write down four things:

  • The goal: more reviews, more qualified leads, better visibility, or something else.
  • The budget ceiling: a fixed amount for one cycle, not an open commitment.
  • The evaluation window: a defined period, such as one full cycle.
  • The exit rule: the result below which you stop.

The guide’s keep-or-de-prioritize logic applies directly. Keep spending where quality and assist signals are stable or improving, and stop where they stay weak after a full cycle.

Watch for Hidden Costs

The listing fee is rarely the only cost. There is the time to write and maintain complete profiles, the ongoing effort of collecting reviews, the support capacity needed for extra leads, and the operational work of keeping information consistent across platforms. This is why the guide stresses that platform count should stay proportional to your ability to maintain quality.

A Note on Submission Services

Some teams prefer to pay for execution rather than placement. ListingBott, for example, offers a one-time payment model, publication to 100+ directories, and a workflow built on a client form, an agreed directory list, and a final report. It also states clearly that it does not guarantee ranking position, traffic by a specific date, indexing speed, or third-party platform outcomes. That is a different kind of spend from paid directory placement, and it fits best when your target list is already clear.

The Bottom Line

Free listings are where every program should start, and paid options are where evidence should lead. Complete your profiles, collect reviews, measure engaged traffic and assists, and only then decide where money can amplify something that already works. Spending to fix a weak profile is the most expensive way to learn it was weak.
